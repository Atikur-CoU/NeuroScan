# Accuracy Improvement Options — Patient-Level Split Model

**Baseline to beat:** 91.47% test accuracy (fresh-init ResNet18, DWI/ADC pairing, patient-level split, n=7,152). Per-class recall: normal 93.08%, ischemic 82.11%, hemorrhagic 98.47%. Dominant error: ischemic↔normal confusion (319 ischemic→normal, 254 normal→ischemic).

**Note:** this baseline and Rounds 1–9 below all ran on the split later found to still have undetected leakage in RSNA_ICH (46% of rows), MRI_Images (99% of rows), and Kaggle_CT (4.1% of rows) — see "Leakage audit round 2" row below and the internal review notes. The split has since been rebuilt (`patient-split-v2`) with zero leakage verified across all 7 datasets; Round 10 re-establishes the baseline on the corrected split.

**Direction shift after Round 10:** confusion-matrix analysis showed ischemic errors are 99.7% confused with "normal" specifically (not hemorrhagic — that boundary is essentially solved). Effort has shifted from generic architecture/loss tuning to diagnosing and fixing the normal-vs-ischemic boundary specifically — see the lesion-size-stratified audit row below and the internal review notes's "Normal-vs-ischemic boundary investigation" section.

**Reference points:** Round 12 (92.10% accuracy, 81.56% ischemic recall, 85.76% ischemic precision) vs. Round 15 (91.14% accuracy, **84.01% ischemic recall** — highest recall to date, but trades accuracy/precision for it) vs. **Round 17 (92.25% accuracy, 83.23% ischemic recall, 84.96% ischemic precision) — best balanced result: beats Round 12 on accuracy AND recall simultaneously, with only a small precision cost.** Round 16 (λ_attn=0.02, weaker gating) tested whether Round 15's tradeoff could be eased — instead it reversed direction, landing at 91.39% accuracy / 76.67% ischemic recall (worse recall than even Round 12). Round 15 still holds the single highest recall; Round 17 is the best result on combined accuracy+recall+precision balance.

**Target (user directive): ischemic recall must cross 90%.**
